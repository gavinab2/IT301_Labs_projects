Helpful advice for getting the best shots, whether you are behind the lens or preparing for your upcoming session.

Behind the Lens: Shooting Tips

Action & Sports

Freeze the Motion

When shooting high-energy events or sports, keep your shutter speed at 1/1000s or faster. Pair that with continuous autofocus (AI Servo or AF-C) and burst mode so you never miss the peak moment of action.

Lighting

Chase the Golden Hour

The hour just after sunrise and the hour right before sunset give you the softest, most flattering natural light. If you have to shoot at midday, look for open shade near buildings or trees to avoid harsh shadows on faces.

Portraits

Create Depth with Aperture

To get that clean, blurry background that makes your subject pop, shoot with a wide aperture like f/1.8 or f/2.8. Also, have your subject step a few feet away from the background to make the separation even stronger.

Post-Processing

Always Shoot in RAW

JPEG files compress your data and lock in your white balance. Shooting in RAW saves all the sensor information, giving you way more flexibility to recover blown-out highlights or brighten dark shadows when editing.

Prepping for Your Portrait Session

1. Coordinate, Don't Match

Pick 2 to 3 complementary colors rather than having everyone wear the exact same outfit. Solid colors and subtle textures photograph much cleaner than busy patterns or large logos.

2. Steam Your Gear Early

Especially for graduation portraits, take your gown and stole out of the packaging a few days early and steam out the creases. Wrinkles on shiny fabric catch the light and are tough to edit out.

3. Bring Comfortable Shoes

Campus and outdoor shoots usually involve a lot of walking between spots. Bring a pair of sneakers or slides to walk in, and swap into your dress shoes right when we set up for the shot.
